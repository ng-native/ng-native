`@ng-native/web-compat` gives a library the DOM's events. `MouseEvent`, `PointerEvent`, `KeyboardEvent`, `FocusEvent`, `TouchEvent`, `Touch` and the other kinds are there with the members their init dictionaries give, `document.createEvent` makes each with its `init*Event`, `window.event` is the event a listener is being told, and an element has its handler properties, `onclick` to `onload`. A press reaches a listener as a `PointerEvent`, a touch is the mouse going down and up as a phone's browser tells it, and a finger held still is a `contextmenu`. A click does what it does in a browser, from a press, a script or `element.click()`: a checkbox or a radio is checked and says `input` and `change`, a button submits or resets its form, and a label clicks or focuses its control. `focus()` and `blur()` take effect at once, on a button, a link or what has a `tabindex` as on a text field, with `document.activeElement`, `:focus`, and `focusin` and `focusout` heard on the document. A button and a link take a press with no listener, and are `:active` while held, `data-hover` while a pointer is over them, and announced as disabled while they are.
